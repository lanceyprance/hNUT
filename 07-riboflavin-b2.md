## The big idea

Riboflavin is the "**yellow**" vitamin (Latin *flavus* = yellow). It's why high-dose B vitamins turn your urine bright yellow.

It forms two coenzymes, **FMN** and **FAD**.
They're **electron carriers**: they pick up electrons (as **FADH₂**) in one reaction and drop them off in another.
That makes riboflavin central to the **electron transport chain**, the **TCA cycle** and **burning fat**.

Run short and the fast-growing tissues of the **mouth** show it first: sore, cracked lips and tongue.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Coenzymes:** **FMN** (flavin mononucleotide) and **FAD** (flavin adenine dinucleotide).

**Structure:** a flavin ring + **ribitol** (from reduced **ribose**). *Flavus* = yellow.

**Job:** **electron carrier** in oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions: **FAD → FADH₂**.
Used in the **electron transport chain**, **fatty acid (β-) oxidation**, **amino acid oxidation**, **TCA cycle**.
Also the antioxidant enzyme **glutathione reductase**.

**Deficiency:** inflammatory lesions of the **mouth**: **glossitis** (tongue), **cheilosis** and **stomatitis** (lips and mouth). Cracked lips, sore throat, skin problems.

**Sources:** **milk and dairy**, eggs, almonds, meat, liver, fish, fortified cereals, green veg.

## 1. Structure

![Riboflavin structure](../img/07/l14-14.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:**

- The three-ring part at the bottom is the **flavin** (that's what's yellow).
- The chain on top, circled in red, is **ribitol**. It comes from the **reduction of ribose** (a sugar). "Ribo" + "flavin" = riboflavin.

**Riboflavin is a component of two coenzymes:**
- **FMN**: flavin mononucleotide (riboflavin + one phosphate)
- **FAD**: flavin adenine dinucleotide (FMN + an adenine nucleotide)

## 3. Function: electron carrier

Riboflavin's coenzymes are **electron carriers** in **oxidation and reduction reactions**.
These are central to metabolism:

1. **Mitochondrial electron transport chain** (ETC)
2. **Fatty acid oxidation** (β-oxidation)
3. **Amino acid oxidation**
4. **TCA cycle**

Plus one non-energy job:
5. **Glutathione reductase**, an **antioxidant enzyme** (it recycles glutathione).

<br>

![FAD in metabolism](../img/07/l14-17.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** the same energy map as Topic 6. **Grey dots = riboflavin.**
Find them on **β-oxidation** (fat breakdown, top right), in the **TCA cycle**, and feeding the **ETC** at the bottom.

<br>

**The key reaction:**

**FAD + 2H → FADH₂**

FAD picks up electrons (and hydrogens) from β-oxidation and the TCA cycle.
**FADH₂** carries them to the **electron transport chain**.
The ETC uses them to make **ATP**.

<br>

**Riboflavin and niacin do the same kind of job.** Both carry electrons:
- B2: **FAD → FADH₂**
- B3: **NAD⁺ → NADH** (Topic 8)

### 🧠 Check
What does FAD do in β-oxidation and the TCA cycle, and where do the electrons end up?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

FAD accepts electrons and becomes FADH₂.

FADH₂ carries them to the electron transport chain, which uses them to make ATP.

</details>

## 4. Deficiency

![Riboflavin deficiency](../img/07/l14-18.jpg)

**Inflammatory lesions of the mouth:**
- **Glossitis**: inflamed, smooth, sore **tongue** (left photo)
- **Cheilosis**: cracks at the **corners of the mouth / lips** (right photo)
- **Stomatitis**: inflammation of the **mouth** lining

Also:
- **Lower metabolic rate**, related to **lipid metabolism** (β-oxidation needs FAD)
- Summary slide: **cracked lips, sore throat, skin disorders**

<br>

**Memory hook:** **B2 = 2 lips.** Riboflavin deficiency shows on the lips and tongue.

### 🧠 Check
Name the three mouth signs of riboflavin deficiency and where each one is.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Glossitis: the tongue.

Cheilosis: the lips and corners of the mouth (cracks).

Stomatitis: the lining of the mouth.

</details>
