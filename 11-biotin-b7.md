## The big idea

Biotin is the **carboxylase** vitamin.
It's the coenzyme for enzymes that **add a carboxyl group (CO₂)** to a molecule.

Those reactions sit at key crossroads: making **new glucose** (gluconeogenesis), making **fatty acids**, and handling **amino acids**.

It's in lots of foods, and your **gut bacteria** make it too, so deficiency is rare.
The classic way to become deficient is eating lots of **raw egg white**: a protein in it, **avidin**, grabs biotin and stops you absorbing it.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Structure:** **ureido ring + thiophene ring + valeric acid** side chain.

**Intake:** EFSA **AI = 40 µg/day**.

**Sources:** **liver, eggs**, fish, wholegrains. Often **bound to protein** in food. Also **made by gut microflora**.

**Job:** coenzyme for **carboxylases** (add a **carboxyl** group):
- **pyruvate carboxylase**: pyruvate → **oxaloacetate** → **gluconeogenesis**
- **acetyl-CoA carboxylase** → **fatty acid synthesis**
- **amino acid** metabolism

**Deficiency (rare):** from **raw egg white (avidin)**. **Dermatitis, hair loss**, lethargy, pins and needles, depression.

## 3. Function: coenzyme for carboxylases

**Carboxylase enzymes** add a **carboxyl group** (–COOH) to a compound.
Biotin is their **coenzyme**: it carries the CO₂ onto the target.

<br>

**Example 1: Pyruvate carboxylase**
**pyruvate + CO₂ → oxaloacetate**
Oxaloacetate is the starting point for **gluconeogenesis** (making new glucose from non-carbohydrate sources).

**Example 2: Acetyl-CoA carboxylase**
**acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → malonyl-CoA**
That's step 1 of **fatty acid synthesis**.
(Same reaction as Topic 9 §4: B5 provides the CoA, biotin does the carboxylation.)

**Example 3: Amino acid metabolism**

<br>

**So biotin is needed for:**
1. **Amino acid** metabolism
2. **Glucose production** (gluconeogenesis)
3. **Fatty acid synthesis**

On the energy map, **dark red/brown dots = biotin**.

<br>

**Memory hook:** **B**iotin **B**rings the **C**O₂. Biotin = **carboxylases**.

## 4. Deficiency

**Rare**, because biotin is widely distributed (and made by gut bacteria).

**The classic cause: lots of raw egg white.**
- Egg white contains a protein called **avidin**.
- Avidin **binds tightly to biotin**.
- That **prevents absorption**.
- Only reported in people who eat **large amounts of uncooked eggs**.
- Cooking **denatures** avidin, so cooked eggs are fine (and are a good source).

<br>

**Symptoms:**
- **dermatitis** (skin)
- **hair loss**
- neurological: **lethargy**, **pins and needles** in the hands and feet, **depression**

<br>

**Memory hook:** **A**vidin is **A**gainst biotin. **H**air and **H**ide (skin) suffer.

### 🧠 Check
A bodybuilder drinks six raw egg whites a day for months and starts losing hair. Explain the link.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Raw egg white contains avidin.

Avidin binds tightly to biotin and stops it being absorbed.

So he's become biotin deficient, and hair loss (plus dermatitis) is a deficiency symptom.

</details>
