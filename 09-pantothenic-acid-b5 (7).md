## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Name, sources and intake (Core Q1)

Where does the name "pantothenic acid" come from, and what does it tell you about its sources? What is the Adequate Intake?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

From the Greek *pantos*, meaning "everywhere".

It's widely distributed in all foods (meats, eggs, yogurt, whole-grain cereals, sweet potatoes, broccoli, mushrooms, fortified cereal).

In food it's free or part of coenzyme A (85% of dietary intake).

AI = 5 mg/day.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Coenzyme A (Core Q2)

Pantothenic acid is part of coenzyme A. List the areas of metabolism that need CoA.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

CoA is critical for ATP production. It's needed for:
- glucose metabolism
- amino acid metabolism
- fatty acid metabolism
- energy (the TCA cycle)

Plus cholesterol and steroid hormone synthesis.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q3: Absorption and metabolism (Core Q3)

Describe how pantothenic acid is absorbed and metabolised. Where is CoA found in highest concentration?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. The CoA form in food is broken down to pantothenic acid.
2. Pantothenic acid is absorbed in the small intestine.
3. It circulates in the blood to the liver, where it's metabolised, then circulated to the rest of the body.
4. Most is used to make CoA.

CoA is found in high concentrations in the liver, heart, kidneys and brain.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q4: CoA as an acyl carrier (Core Q4)

Using one catabolic and one synthesis example, explain how CoA acts as a carrier of fatty acids.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Catabolic (β-oxidation):** when fatty acids are broken down for energy, 2-carbon fragments are cut from the end of the chain. Each fragment joins CoA to form acetyl-CoA, which enters the TCA cycle to produce energy.

**Synthesis:** in long-chain fatty acid synthesis, step 1 is acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → malonyl-CoA. CoA is also needed for cholesterol and steroid hormone synthesis.

From: §4

</details>

### M2: Multiple choice

"Burning feet syndrome" is a sign of deficiency of:

- A. Thiamin
- B. Riboflavin
- C. Pantothenic acid
- D. Biotin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

Numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. Thiamin deficiency causes beriberi, riboflavin causes mouth lesions, biotin causes dermatitis and hair loss.

### M4: Explain

Explain why pantothenic acid deficiency produces a "general failure of body systems" rather than one specific sign. List the symptoms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Pantothenic acid is part of CoA, which is needed in almost every energy pathway (glucose, fatty acid, amino acid metabolism and the TCA cycle) and for ATP production. So a shortage affects many systems at once.

Symptoms: fatigue, depression and irritability; gastrointestinal stress, vomiting and cramps; "burning feet syndrome" (numbness and tingling of the hands and feet).

From: §4, §5

</details>
