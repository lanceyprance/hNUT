## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Folate vs folic acid (Core Q1)

What's the difference between folate and folic acid? Name the three parts all folates share, and compare their bioavailability.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Folate is the natural form. Folic acid is the synthetic form.

Three parts: pteridine, PABA (para-aminobenzoic acid), glutamic acid. 80% of dietary folates are polyglutamates.

Bioavailability: about 50% of dietary folate is available; about 100% of synthetic free folic acid.

From: §1, §3

</details>

### Q2: Absorption and metabolism (Core Q2)

Describe the absorption and metabolism of folate up to 5-methyl-THF.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Hydrolysed in the small intestine by conjugase, a zinc-dependent enzyme.
2. Absorbed by active transport.
3. Within the intestinal cell, reduced to dihydrofolate (DHF) then tetrahydrofolate (THF) by reductase, using NADPH. Four hydrogens are added.
4. THF is methylated and enters the bloodstream as 5-methyl-THF.
5. Circulates to the liver, then the rest of the body.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q3: Single-carbon transfers (Core Q3)

Folate facilitates single-carbon transfers. Name the four forms of folate and the one-carbon unit each carries. Give one example of a reaction in DNA synthesis.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

5-methyl-THF: methyl, –CH₃.

5,10-methylene-THF: methylene, –CH₂–.

5,10-methenyl-THF: methenyl, –CH=.

10-formyl-THF: formyl, O=CH–.

Example: 5,10-methylene-THF is needed to convert deoxyuridine monophosphate (dUMP) to thymidylate (dTMP), for DNA synthesis.

From: §5, §6

</details>

---

### Q4: Homocysteine (Core Q4)

Explain the roles of folate and vitamin B12 in converting homocysteine to methionine. Why is methionine important?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

5-methyl-THF transfers its methyl group (–CH₃) to vitamin B12, leaving THF.

Methylated vitamin B12 transfers the methyl group to homocysteine, forming methionine.

Methionine is required for DNA methylation, which controls gene expression.

From: §6, §7

</details>

### Q5: Deficiency (Core Q5)

Explain why folate deficiency causes megaloblastic anaemia, and name the other tissues most affected.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Folate is needed to make thymidine for DNA. Cells that divide rapidly have a large requirement.

Without folate, DNA synthesis fails and cell division slows, but RNA synthesis continues, so cells grow large with large nuclei.

Red blood cells end up large (macrocytic), irregular and often nucleated: megaloblastic anaemia.

Other tissues: bone marrow, intestine, hair follicles.

From: §8

</details>

### Q6: Calculation: DFE (Core Q6)

A woman's diet gives 180 µg natural food folate and 150 µg folic acid from fortified bread. She also takes a 400 µg folic acid supplement.

1. Find her total DFE.
2. Does she meet the pregnancy PRI of 600 µg/day?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**1.**

Synthetic folic acid = 150 + 400 = 550 µg

DFE = 180 + (1.7 × 550) = 180 + 935 = **1115 µg DFE**

**2.**

Yes. 1115 µg is well above 600 µg.

From: §3 Equation 1, §2

</details>

To prevent a first neural tube defect, Irish women of childbearing age are advised to take:

- A. 400 µg folic acid daily before conception and for the first 12 weeks
- B. 4 mg folic acid daily for the first 4 months
- C. 150 µg folic acid from fortified bread only
- D. 600 µg folic acid only after the pregnancy is confirmed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

B is the advice to prevent a recurrence. Starting after the pregnancy is confirmed is too late: the neural tube forms very early.

### M4: Calculation: food vs supplement

How many µg of natural food folate would you need to match the DFE of a 400 µg folic acid supplement?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

DFE of the supplement = 1.7 × 400 = 680 µg DFE

Food folate counts 1 : 1, so you'd need **680 µg** food folate.

From: §3 Equation 1

</details>

### M5: Explain

Summarise the history of mandatory folic acid fortification in Ireland from 2006 to 2016.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

2006: the National Committee on Folic Acid Food Fortification recommended mandatory fortification of most white, brown and wholemeal bread.

2009: the Implementation Group decided there would be no public health benefit at that time, because intake had already increased through voluntary fortification and there was some evidence for negative health effects.

2016: FSAI (Option 1): mandatory fortification of bread or flour to give about 150 µg/day to women of childbearing age could cut NTDs by about 30%, alongside advice to take an extra 400 µg supplement; voluntary fortification would continue.

(In the US and Canada, mandatory flour enrichment reduced NTDs by 20–25%.)

From: §11

</details>

### M6: Explain

A woman who had a previous pregnancy affected by a neural tube defect is planning to conceive. What should she take, and why must she start before conception?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

4 mg (4000 µg) folic acid daily, from at least 1 month before conception and for the first 4 months of pregnancy.

Folate is needed for growth of nerve tissue in the foetus. The neural tube forms and closes very early, often before a woman knows she's pregnant, so folate status must already be high at conception.

From: §9, §10

</details>
