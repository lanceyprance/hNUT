## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Structure and sources (Core Q1)

Name the three structural parts of biotin. Give its main sources and its Adequate Intake.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ureido ring, thiophene ring, valeric acid side chain.

Sources: widely distributed: liver, eggs, fish and wholegrains. Present in many foods bound to protein. Also synthesised by intestinal microflora.

AI = 40 µg/day (EFSA).

From: §1, §2

</details>

---

### Q2: Function (Core Q2)

Explain the function of biotin, using pyruvate carboxylase as an example. Name the three areas of metabolism that need it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Biotin is the coenzyme for carboxylase enzymes, which add a carboxyl group to a compound.

Example: pyruvate carboxylase adds a carboxylic acid group to pyruvate to form oxaloacetate.

Areas: amino acid metabolism, glucose production (gluconeogenesis), fatty acid synthesis (acetyl-CoA carboxylase).

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q3: Deficiency (Core Q3)

Explain the main cause of biotin deficiency and list its symptoms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It's rare, because biotin is widely distributed in foods.

Reported in people who eat large amounts of uncooked eggs. Avidin, a protein in egg white, binds tightly to biotin and prevents its absorption.

Symptoms: dermatitis, hair loss, and neurological symptoms such as lethargy, pins and needles in the extremities, and depression.

From: §4

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Biotin is a coenzyme for carboxylase enzymes.
2. Gut bacteria can make biotin.
3. Avidin is found in egg yolk.
4. Biotin is needed for fatty acid synthesis.
5. Biotin has a PRI of 40 µg/day.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **True.** (§3)
2. **True.** Intestinal microflora. (§2)
3. **False.** Avidin is in egg white. (§4)
4. **True.** Via acetyl-CoA carboxylase. (§3)
5. **False.** It's an Adequate Intake (AI) of 40 µg/day, not a PRI. (§2)

### M4: Explain

Someone regularly eats raw egg whites. Explain why they may become biotin deficient even though eggs are listed as a good biotin source.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Egg white contains avidin, a protein that binds tightly to biotin and prevents its absorption.

So with large amounts of uncooked egg, the biotin in the diet can't be absorbed.

Cooked eggs are a good source: the problem is raw egg white.

From: §2, §4

</details>

---

### M5: Explain

Explain why biotin is important for blood glucose during fasting.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Biotin is the coenzyme for pyruvate carboxylase, which turns pyruvate into oxaloacetate.

Oxaloacetate is the start of gluconeogenesis, making new glucose.

So biotin is needed for glucose production.

From: §3

</details>
