## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Grouping the B vitamins (Core Q1)

The B complex vitamins can be grouped by function. Name the three groups and give two vitamins in each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Energy:** thiamin (B1), riboflavin (B2), niacin (B3), pantothenic acid (B5), biotin (B7), vitamin B6.

**Haematopoietic** (with vitamin C): folate (B9), vitamin B12, vitamin B6, pantothenic acid (B5).

**Other:** vitamin B6, thiamin (B1), folate (B9), vitamin B12, niacin (B3).

### Q2: Thiamin's functions (Core Q2)

Name the active form of thiamin and describe its three functions.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Active form: thiamine diphosphate (TDP), also called TPP.

1. **Energy metabolism** (coenzyme): oxidative decarboxylation reactions, e.g. pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, branched-chain keto-acid dehydrogenase.
2. **Synthesis of DNA/RNA and NADPH** (coenzyme): transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway. NADPH is used for fatty acid synthesis.
3. **Nerve conduction** (non-coenzyme): needed for the nerve membrane Na transport protein.

From: §2, §4, §5

</details>

### Q3: Oxidative decarboxylation (Core Q3)

Name the three TDP-dependent enzymes in energy metabolism and the reaction each catalyses. Which other B vitamins do these enzymes need?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Pyruvate dehydrogenase: pyruvate → acetyl-CoA.
2. α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase: α-ketoglutarate → succinyl-CoA.
3. Branched-chain keto-acid dehydrogenase: branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) metabolism.

They also need B2 (as FAD), B3 (as NAD) and B5 (as CoA).

From: §4

</details>

### Q4: Beriberi (Core Q4)

What causes beriberi? Compare dry and wet beriberi.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Chronic thiamin deficiency together with a high carbohydrate intake.

**Dry beriberi** affects the nervous system: weakness, stiffness and cramps in the legs, then muscle wasting and paralysis spreading upward.

**Wet beriberi** affects the heart: arterioles dilate, fast blood flow and pulse, leading to heart failure and oedema.

From: §6

</details>

### Q5: Calculation: thiamin from energy intake (Core Q5)

A student eats 2200 kcal a day. Using a PRI of 0.1 mg thiamin per MJ, find:

1. the energy intake in MJ
2. the thiamin PRI in mg/day

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**1.**

MJ = 2200 × 4.2 ÷ 1000 = **9.24 MJ**

**2.**

PRI = 0.1 × 9.24 = **0.92 mg/day**

### M4: Explain

Explain why alcoholics are at high risk of thiamin deficiency, and describe how the deficiency usually presents in them.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Reasons: low thiamin intake, high carbohydrate intake, alcohol affects thiamin absorption, and liver damage.

It usually presents as Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, with central nervous system lesions:
Korsakoff's psychosis: loss of recent memory (confusion).
Wernicke's encephalopathy: affects vision.

From: §7

</details>

### M5: Explain

Why is the thiamin requirement expressed per MJ of energy rather than as a fixed amount per day?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Thiamin's main job is in energy metabolism: as TDP, it's the coenzyme for oxidative decarboxylation (e.g. pyruvate → acetyl-CoA), especially in carbohydrate breakdown.

The more energy (especially carbohydrate) you eat, the more of these reactions happen, so the more thiamin you need.

From: §3, §4

</details>
