## The big idea

Niacin makes **NAD** and **NADP**, two of the busiest coenzymes in the body (200+ enzymes).

**NAD⁺** is an electron **acceptor** when you **break down** fuel for energy.
**NADPH** is an electron **donor** when you **build** things, like fatty acids.

You can also make niacin from the amino acid **tryptophan** (about 60 mg tryptophan → 1 mg niacin). So protein counts towards your niacin.

Deficiency is **pellagra**, the "4 Ds". It hit populations living on **maize**, which is low in tryptophan and has its niacin locked up.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Forms:** **nicotinic acid** and **nicotinamide**. Precursor to **NAD** and **NADP**.

**Niacin equivalents:**

**1 mg NE = 1 mg preformed niacin = 60 mg tryptophan**

**NE (mg) = mg niacin + mg tryptophan ÷ 60**

<br>

**Jobs:**
- **NAD⁺**: electron acceptor in **energy-yielding** (oxidative) reactions → glycolysis, pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, β-oxidation, TCA cycle, ETC → ATP
- **NADPH**: **reductive biosynthesis** → **fatty acid synthesis**, **folate metabolism**
- Non-redox: **DNA repair**

**Deficiency:** **pellagra** ("rough skin") = **4 Ds**: **dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia, death**.

**Sources:** meat, poultry (~10 mg), fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, nuts, fortified cereals.

## 1. Pellagra and maize

![Pellagra and maize](../img/08/l14-19.jpg)

**Maize (corn) is low in tryptophan.**
So you can't make much niacin from it.

**The niacin in cereals like maize is unavailable** (bound up, so you can't absorb it).

So populations living mainly on maize were at high risk of **pellagra**. The left photo shows the rough, scaly skin on the legs.

## 2. Structure

![Niacin structure](../img/08/l14-20.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:**

- **Nicotinic acid** (top left): a **pyridine** ring with a **carboxyl group (–COOH) at position 3**.
- **Nicotinamide** (top middle): same, but the carboxyl is replaced by a **carboxamide group (–CONH₂)**.
- Bottom: **NAD** (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide). Nicotinamide is the working end.
- **NADP** is the **same structure as NAD**, with one extra **phosphate** (bottom right of the slide).

**Niacin is the precursor to the coenzyme NAD.**

## 3. Foods and niacin equivalents

- Dairy products, eggs
- **Enriched breads and cereals** (fortified)
- Fish, **lean meat** (beef)
- **Poultry** (chicken, turkey) ≈ **10 mg** per portion
- Legumes, nuts

<br>

### 📐 Equation 1: Niacin Equivalents (NE)

**1 mg niacin equivalent = mg preformed niacin + (1/60 × mg tryptophan)**

| Symbol | Means | Units |
|---|---|---|
| NE | niacin equivalents: total niacin value | mg |
| preformed niacin | niacin already in the food | mg |
| tryptophan | amino acid in the food's protein; 60 mg makes 1 mg niacin | mg |

**What it means:**
The body converts tryptophan to niacin, but only **1 mg niacin from every 60 mg tryptophan**.
So a protein-rich food is worth more niacin than its label says.

**Use it when:**
You're given niacin **and** tryptophan for a food or diet, and asked for total niacin.

**Watch out:**
Divide the tryptophan by 60. Don't add it straight on.

<br>

**Requirement:** the slide gives **PRI 1.3–1.6 mg/MJ**.
(EFSA's niacin PRI is **1.6 mg NE per MJ**.) So like thiamin (Topic 6), it scales with **energy intake**:

**PRI (mg NE/day) = 1.6 × energy intake (MJ/day)**

### 🧠 Check
A meal has 6 mg niacin and 600 mg tryptophan. How many mg NE?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

NE = 6 + (600 ÷ 60) = 6 + 10 = **16 mg NE**

</details>

## 4. Function: NAD and NADP

Niacin's coenzymes work with **200+ enzymes**.

**1. Oxidative reactions (breaking down) → NAD⁺**
NAD⁺ is the **electron acceptor** in **energy-yielding metabolism**.
It becomes **NADH + H⁺**.

**2. Reductive biosynthesis (building up) → NADPH**
NADPH is the **major coenzyme for reductive reactions**:
- required for **fatty acid synthesis**
- **folate metabolism** (Topic 12: reducing folate to THF uses NADPH)

**3. Non-redox roles**
- involved in **DNA repair**

<br>

**Memory hook:** NAD⁺ **takes** electrons when you **break down** fuel. NADP**H** **hands** them over when you **build** (the P is for "**P**roduce").

### 🧠 Check
Which niacin coenzyme is used in fatty acid synthesis, and is that an oxidative or reductive role?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

NADPH.

It's a reductive role: NADPH donates electrons in reductive biosynthesis.

</details>

## 5. Niacin in energy metabolism

![NAD in energy metabolism](../img/08/l14-23.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** the same energy map as Topics 6 and 7. **Green dots = niacin.**
Green dots are **everywhere**: glycolysis, pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, β-oxidation, all through the TCA cycle.

**The key reaction:**

**NAD⁺ + 2H → NADH + H⁺**

NAD⁺ collects electrons from:
- **glycolysis**
- **pyruvate → acetyl-CoA**
- **β-oxidation**
- the **TCA cycle**

**NADH** carries them to the **electron transport chain** → **ATP**.

<br>

**Compare with riboflavin (Topic 7):** FAD → FADH₂ does the same job. Both are electron carriers that feed the ETC.

## 6. Deficiency: pellagra

**Pellagra** = "rough skin" (Italian *pelle agra*).

**The 4 Ds:**
1. **Dermatitis**: rough, scaly skin, especially where the sun hits
2. **Diarrhoea**
3. **Dementia**
4. **Death**

They happen in that order if it's not treated.

<br>

**Who's at risk:** people living on **maize** (low tryptophan, unavailable niacin, §1).
