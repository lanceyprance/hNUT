<details><summary>Q2 answers</summary>

**a.** 60 + (1800 ÷ 12) + (720 ÷ 24) = 60 + 150 + 30 = **240 µg RAE**. 240 ÷ 700 × 100 = **34%**.
→ Topic 2 §3

**b.** 600 ÷ 40 = **15 µg**. Yes: housebound older adults should take 15 µg (600 IU) daily, all year.
→ Topic 4 §5, §10

**c.** 25 + (0.5 × 40) + (0.1 × 30) + (0.3 × 10) = 25 + 20 + 3 + 3 = **51 mg α-TE per 100 g**.
→ Topic 5 §2

**d.** 2600 × 4.2 ÷ 1000 = 10.92 MJ. PRI = 0.1 × 10.92 = **1.09 mg/day**.
→ Topic 6 §3

**e.** 12 + (720 ÷ 60) = 12 + 12 = **24 mg NE**.
→ Topic 8 §3

**f.** 15 × 75 = 1125 µg = **1.13 mg/day**.
→ Topic 10 §2

**g.** 200 + (1.7 × 400) = 200 + 680 = **880 µg DFE**.
→ Topic 12 §3

</details>

<details><summary>Q3 answers</summary>

**a.** Poor absorption; poor intestinal activity of carotene dioxygenase; other carotenoids may inhibit carotene dioxygenase; cleavage at other points gives 8-, 10-, 12-apo-carotenals that are oxidised to retinoic acid, not retinol.
→ Topic 2 §3

**b.** 11-cis-retinal binds opsin to form rhodopsin. Light converts it to all-trans-retinal, which splits from opsin and sends a signal to the brain. All-trans-retinal is converted back to cis-retinal and rhodopsin is reformed (or converted to retinoic acid). In deficiency, there isn't enough retinal to restore cis-retinal, so dark adaptation is slow and vision in poor light is impaired.
→ Topic 2 §6

**c.** Night blindness → xerophthalmia (dry eye, inadequate mucus, Bitot's spots) → irreversible blindness.
→ Topic 2 §8

**d.** Any two: liver biopsy, dark adaptation time, Bitot's spots, Relative Dose Response test.
→ Topic 2 §9

</details>

<details><summary>Q4 answers</summary>

**a.** A molecule with an unpaired electron, highly reactive. It steals an electron from a stable molecule, which becomes a new radical and does the same: a cascade or chain reaction.
→ Topic 5 §4

**b.** Vitamin E sits in membranes and limits radical damage from oxidation of PUFAs. It donates a hydrogen from its phenolic –OH group to neutralise the radical and end the chain. It becomes a tocopheroxyl radical, stabilised by its aromatic ring (resonance). Vitamin C reduces it back to tocopherol.
→ Topic 5 §5

**c.** Any five: low antioxidant intake, age-related decline in natural defences, toxins, cigarettes, poor nutrition, stress, UV light.
→ Topic 5 §7

</details>

<details><summary>Q6 answers</summary>

**a.** Alanine + α-ketoglutarate → pyruvate + glutamate. Vitamin B6, as PLP, the coenzyme for transaminases.
→ Topic 10 §4

**b.** Pyruvate carboxylase (pyruvate → oxaloacetate). Supports gluconeogenesis (glucose production).
→ Topic 11 §3

**c.** Polyglutamates are hydrolysed by conjugase (zinc-dependent), absorbed by active transport, reduced in the intestinal cell to DHF then THF by reductase using NADPH (four hydrogens added), then THF is methylated and enters the blood as 5-methyl-THF.
→ Topic 12 §4
