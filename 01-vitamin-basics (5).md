## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: What makes a vitamin a vitamin? (Core Q1)

List the five features that define a vitamin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Most are not made in the body, so they're needed from the diet.
2. Deficiency causes disease, and repletion cures it ("vital amine").
3. They have no common chemical structure.
4. They don't yield energy.
5. They're needed in small amounts (µg or mg).

From: §3

</details>

### Q2: Fat-soluble vs water-soluble (Core Q2)

Compare fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins under these headings: absorption, transport, storage, excretion, toxicity, how often they're needed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Absorption:** water-soluble go directly into the blood. Fat-soluble go into the lymph first, then the blood.

**Transport:** water-soluble travel freely. Many fat-soluble need carrier proteins.

**Storage:** water-soluble circulate and aren't stored. Fat-soluble are stored in fat tissue (and liver).

**Excretion:** water-soluble excess is removed by the kidneys in urine. Fat-soluble are less readily excreted and stay in fat stores.

**Toxicity:** possible for water-soluble at very high doses. Likely for fat-soluble at very high doses.

**Requirements:** water-soluble are needed more often. Fat-soluble less often, because of storage.

From: §4

</details>

### Q3: Dietary Reference Values (Core Q3)

Define the Average Requirement (AR), the Population Reference Intake (PRI) and the Adequate Intake (AI).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**AR:** the intake that's adequate for half of the people in a population group, assuming requirements are normally distributed.

**PRI:** the intake that's adequate for virtually all people in a population group.

**AI:** used when a PRI can't be set because an AR can't be determined. It's the average observed daily intake of a healthy population, assumed to be adequate.

From: §5

</details>

### Q4: Micronutrients and bone (Core Q4)

Name three vitamins involved in bone health and give the role of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Vitamin D: helps maintain calcium balance.

Vitamin K: involved with enzymes in bone formation.

Vitamin C: involved with enzymes in bone formation (collagen).

From: §2

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Vitamins are a source of energy.
2. Vitamin A, D, E and K are the fat-soluble vitamins.
3. Water-soluble vitamins are absorbed into the lymph before the blood.
4. Fat-soluble vitamins are more likely to reach toxic levels than water-soluble vitamins.
5. The PRI covers about half of the population.
6. An Adequate Intake is set when there isn't enough data to set an Average Requirement.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **False.** Vitamins don't yield energy. (§3)
2. **True.** Remember DEKA. (§4)
3. **False.** Water-soluble go directly into the blood. Fat-soluble go via the lymph. (§4)
4. **True.** They're stored in fat and less readily excreted. (§4)
5. **False.** The AR covers half. The PRI covers virtually all. (§5)
6. **True.** That's exactly when an AI is used. (§5)

</details>

Which vitamin is matched with the wrong chemical name?

- A. Vitamin E: tocopherol
- B. Vitamin B1: riboflavin
- C. Vitamin B12: cobalamin
- D. Vitamin D: calciferol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

B1 is thiamin. Riboflavin is B2.

### M3: Multiple choice

Why do fat-soluble vitamins need to be eaten less often than water-soluble ones?

- A. They're absorbed more efficiently
- B. They're stored in fat tissue and the liver
- C. The body can make all of them
- D. They're excreted faster in urine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

Fat-soluble vitamins are stored, so the body can draw on its stores between meals.

From: §4

</details>

### M4: Explain

A friend says: "Vitamin C is water-soluble, so you can never take too much of it." Is that right? Explain using what you know about water-soluble vitamins.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Not quite.

It's true that water-soluble vitamins aren't stored, and the kidneys remove excess in the urine, so toxicity is less likely than for fat-soluble vitamins.

But toxic levels are still **possible** when very high doses are consumed.

From: §4

</details>

### M5: Explain

"Vitamin" comes from "vital amine". Explain what "vital" refers to.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Vitamins are vital for life: deficiency of a vitamin causes a specific disease, and giving it back (repletion) cures that disease.

Most can't be made by the body, so they must come from the diet.

From: §3

</details>
