## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Forms and coenzymes (Core Q1)

Name the two forms of niacin and the two coenzymes it forms. How do nicotinic acid and nicotinamide differ?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Nicotinic acid and nicotinamide.

Coenzymes: NAD (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) and NADP.

Nicotinic acid is a pyridine with a carboxyl group at position 3. In nicotinamide, the carboxyl group is replaced by a carboxamide group (–CONH₂).

NADP has the same structure as NAD but is phosphorylated.

From: §2

</details>

### Q2: Functions (Core Q2)

Describe the three types of metabolic function of niacin's coenzymes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Coenzymes for 200+ enzymes:

1. **Oxidative reactions:** NAD⁺ is the electron acceptor in energy-yielding metabolism (glycolysis, pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, β-oxidation, TCA cycle). NADH carries electrons to the ETC to make ATP.
2. **Reductive biosynthesis:** NADPH is the major coenzyme for reductive reactions, required for fatty acid synthesis and folate metabolism.
3. **Non-redox roles:** involved in DNA repair.

From: §4, §5

</details>

### Q3: Pellagra (Core Q3)

Describe pellagra and explain why it was linked to diets based on maize.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Pellagra (rough skin) is niacin deficiency. Its symptoms are the 4 Ds: dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia, death.

Maize (corn) is low in tryptophan, so little niacin can be made from it. And the niacin in cereals is unavailable.

From: §1, §6

</details>

### Q4: Calculation: niacin equivalents (Core Q4)

A day's diet contains 14 mg preformed niacin and 900 mg tryptophan.

Find the total niacin equivalents.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Step 1: tryptophan to niacin.**

900 ÷ 60 = 15 mg

**Step 2: add the preformed niacin.**

NE = 14 + 15 = **29 mg NE**

From: §3 Equation 1

</details>

---

### Q5: Sources (Core Q5)

Give four good food sources of niacin. Why does protein intake matter for niacin status?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any four: dairy products, eggs, enriched (fortified) breads and cereals, fish, lean meats (beef), legumes, nuts, poultry (about 10 mg per portion).

Protein contains tryptophan, and the body can make niacin from tryptophan (60 mg tryptophan → 1 mg niacin). So protein adds to niacin equivalents.

From: §3

</details>

### M4: Calculation: niacin from a chicken dinner

A chicken dinner provides 10 mg preformed niacin and 360 mg tryptophan.

1. Find the niacin equivalents.
2. A woman eating 8 MJ a day needs 1.6 mg NE per MJ. What % of her daily need does the dinner give?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**1.**

NE = 10 + (360 ÷ 60) = 10 + 6 = **16 mg NE**

**2.**

Need = 1.6 × 8 = 12.8 mg NE

% = 16 ÷ 12.8 × 100 = **125%**

From: §3 Equation 1

</details>

### M5: Explain

Compare the roles of NAD⁺ and NADPH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

NAD⁺ is an electron acceptor in oxidative, energy-yielding reactions (glycolysis, pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, β-oxidation, TCA cycle). It becomes NADH, which takes the electrons to the ETC to make ATP.

NADPH is an electron donor in reductive biosynthesis: it's needed for fatty acid synthesis and folate metabolism.

So NAD⁺ is mainly for breaking down, NADPH for building up.

From: §4, §5

</details>
