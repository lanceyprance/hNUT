## The big idea

Folate moves **single carbon units** (like a methyl group, CH₃) from one molecule to another.

That matters most for **making DNA**. So cells that **divide fast** suffer first when folate runs low:
- **red blood cells** → **megaloblastic anaemia**
- the growing **baby's nervous system** → **neural tube defects** (spina bifida, anencephaly)

That's why **every woman who could become pregnant** is told to take **400 µg folic acid** a day, starting **before** conception.

**Folic acid** (synthetic, in supplements and fortified foods) is absorbed far better than natural **folate**. **DFE** puts them on the same scale.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Folate = natural form. Folic acid = synthetic form.**
3 parts: **pteridine + PABA + glutamic acid**. **80%** of dietary folates are **polyglutamates**.

**Intake:** EFSA PRI **330 µg/day**, **600 µg/day in pregnancy**.

**Bioavailability:** food folate **~50%**, synthetic folic acid **~100%**.

**DFE = µg food folate + 1.7 × µg synthetic folic acid**
(1 µg DFE = 1 µg food folate = 0.6 µg folic acid)

<br>

**Metabolism:** **conjugase** (zinc-dependent) → **active transport** → reduced to **DHF → THF** (NADPH) → methylated to **5-methyl-THF** → blood → liver → body.

**Active form:** **tetrahydrofolate (THF)**.

**Job:** **single-carbon transfers** → **DNA/RNA synthesis** (dUMP → dTMP), **amino acid** metabolism (homocysteine → methionine, with **B12**), neurotransmitters.

**Deficiency:** **megaloblastic anaemia**. In pregnancy: **neural tube defects**.

**Ireland:** **400 µg folic acid/day before conception + first 12 weeks.**
After a previous NTD: **4 mg (4000 µg)/day**, from 1 month before conception through the **first 4 months**.

## 1. Folate vs folic acid

![Folate structure](../img/12/l15-20.jpg)

- **Folate**: the **natural** form, in food.
- **Folic acid**: the **synthetic** form, in supplements and fortified foods.

<br>

**How to read this diagram:** folate is a group of related compounds that all have **3 parts**, left to right:
1. **Pteridine** (the double ring)
2. **PABA** (para-aminobenzoic acid, the single ring)
3. **Glutamic acid**

Pteridine + PABA = pteroic acid. Add one glutamic acid = folate (pteroylmonoglutamate).

**80% of dietary folates are polyglutamates**: they have a **chain** of several glutamates, not just one.
The gut has to trim them before absorption (§4).

## 3. Bioavailability and DFE

- About **50%** of **dietary folate** is available.
- About **100%** of **synthetic free folic acid** is available.

So 1 µg of folic acid does about **1.7×** as much as 1 µg of food folate.

<br>

### 📐 Equation 1: Dietary Folate Equivalents (DFE)

**DFE (µg) = µg food folate + (1.7 × µg synthetic folic acid)**

Same thing another way:
**1 µg DFE = 1 µg mixed food folate = 0.6 µg free folic acid**

| Symbol | Means | Units |
|---|---|---|
| DFE | dietary folate equivalents: folate value adjusted for absorption | µg |
| food folate | natural folate in food | µg |
| folic acid | synthetic, from fortified food or supplements | µg |
| 1.7 | folic acid counts 1.7× (because 1 ÷ 0.6 ≈ 1.7) | — |

**What it means:**
Folic acid is absorbed much better than food folate, so it counts for more.

**Use it when:**
A diet has **both** natural folate and fortified foods or supplements, and you need the total.

**Watch out:**
Only the **synthetic folic acid** gets the 1.7. Natural food folate counts 1 : 1.

### 🧠 Check
A woman eats 250 µg food folate and takes a 400 µg folic acid supplement. Find her DFE. Does she meet the pregnancy PRI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

DFE = 250 + (1.7 × 400) = 250 + 680 = **930 µg DFE**

Yes. The pregnancy PRI is 600 µg/day.

</details>

## 4. Absorption and metabolism

1. **Hydrolysed** in the small intestine by **conjugase**, a **zinc-dependent** enzyme.
   (It trims polyglutamates down to monoglutamates.)
2. **Absorbed by active transport.**
3. Converted to **tetrahydrofolate (THF)**, then a **methyl group (CH₃)** is added → **5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate**.
4. Circulates to the **liver**, then to the rest of the body.

<br>

![Reduction and methylation](../img/12/l15-24.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** inside the intestinal cell, go top to bottom.

1. **Folate → dihydrofolate (DHF)**: reductase, using **NADPH** (niacin! Topic 8)
2. **DHF → tetrahydrofolate (THF)**: reductase again, NADPH again
3. In total, **four hydrogens** are added ("tetra-hydro")
4. **THF is methylated** and enters the blood as **5-methyl-THF**

### 🧠 Check
Put these in order: THF, folate, 5-methyl-THF, DHF. Which coenzyme provides the hydrogens?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Folate → DHF → THF → 5-methyl-THF.

NADPH provides the hydrogens (via reductase).

</details>

## 5. Function: single-carbon transfers

**Folate facilitates single-carbon transfers** for:
- **amino acid** metabolism
- **DNA and RNA** synthesis
- **neurotransmitters**

<br>

![Forms of folate](../img/12/l15-28.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** THF carries its one-carbon unit on the N at position **5**, position **10**, or bridging both (red circles).

| Form of folate | One-carbon unit carried |
|---|---|
| **5-methyl-THF** | methyl: **–CH₃** |
| **5,10-methylene-THF** | methylene: **–CH₂–** |
| **5,10-methenyl-THF** | methenyl: **–CH=** |
| **10-formyl-THF** | formyl: **O=CH–** |

The numbers in the name tell you which nitrogen(s) hold the carbon.

---

## 6. Folate and DNA synthesis

**1. Making nucleotides** (adenosine, guanine, thymidine, the building blocks of DNA)

The key step:
**5,10-methylene-THF** gives its carbon to turn
**dUMP** (deoxyuridine monophosphate) → **dTMP** (thymidylate) → **DNA synthesis**

**2. Making methionine**
Methionine is needed for **DNA methylation**, which controls **gene expression**.

---

## 7. Folate, B12 and homocysteine

![Folate and amino acid metabolism](../img/12/l15-30.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** it's two linked circles, left to right.

1. **5-methyl-THF** (folate) gives its **methyl group (–CH₃)** to **vitamin B12** (cobalamin).
   Folate is left as plain **THF**.
2. **Methyl-B12** gives that CH₃ to **homocysteine**.
3. Homocysteine becomes **methionine**.

So folate **and** B12 are both needed to turn **homocysteine → methionine**.
Short of either, homocysteine builds up and methionine (for DNA methylation, §6) runs low.

### 🧠 Check
Describe how folate and B12 convert homocysteine to methionine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

5-methyl-THF transfers its methyl group to vitamin B12, leaving THF.

Methylated B12 then transfers the methyl group to homocysteine, forming methionine.

</details>

## 8. Deficiency: megaloblastic anaemia

Folate is needed to make **thymidine for DNA** (§6).
So deficiency hits cells that are **dividing rapidly**:
- **red blood cells** → **megaloblastic anaemia**
- **bone marrow**
- **intestine**
- **hair follicles**

<br>

![Red blood cell formation](../img/12/l15-25.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** left column = normal red cell production. Right column = folate (or B12) deficiency.

**Normal:** DNA synthesis and division → haemoglobin made → nucleus leaves → **small** mature red cells packed with haemoglobin.

**Folate deficiency:**
1. **Without folate, DNA strands break and cell division slows down.**
2. But **RNA synthesis carries on**, so the cell keeps growing.
3. Result: **large** cells with a **large nucleus**.
4. The red cells end up **big (macrocytic)**, **irregular**, and often **still have a nucleus**.

<br>

![Normal vs megaloblastic cells](../img/12/l15-26.jpg)

Left: normal red blood cells. Right: **megaloblastic, macrocytic** red cells. Bigger, fewer, and not working properly.

<br>

**Memory hook:** **mega** = big. No folate → can't copy DNA → cell grows but can't divide → **giant** red cells.

### 🧠 Check
Why does folate deficiency produce large red blood cells instead of normal ones?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Without folate, DNA synthesis fails, so cell division slows down.

RNA synthesis carries on, so the cell keeps growing.

You end up with large (macrocytic, megaloblastic) cells, often still with a nucleus.

</details>

## 9. Folate in pregnancy: neural tube defects

Folate is **required for growth of nerve tissue** in the foetus.
More folate **reduces the risk of neural tube defects (NTDs)**.

![Neural tube development](../img/12/l15-32.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** early in pregnancy, a flat plate of tissue rolls up into a tube. The tube becomes the brain (top) and spinal cord. If it doesn't close fully, you get an NTD.
This happens in the **first weeks**, often before a woman knows she's pregnant. That's why folic acid has to start **before conception**.

<br>

**Two NTDs:**
- **Anencephaly**: the **upper end** of the neural tube fails to close. The **brain fails to develop**.
- **Spina bifida**: **incomplete closure of the spinal cord**. Can be mild or severe.

**Folate supplementation (400 µg/day)** can help **prevent** NTDs and **reduce** their severity.

<br>

![NTD rates Ireland and Europe](../img/12/l15-34.jpg)

**How to read this chart:** NTDs per 1000 births, 2000 to 2011.
Ireland (dark line) is **consistently higher than Europe** (light line), and **rising again** after 2009 (about 1.2 per 1000 by 2011).

## 10. Recommendations in Ireland

**To prevent NTDs:**
All women of **childbearing age** should take:
- **400 µg folic acid daily**
- starting **before conception**
- and for the **first 12 weeks** of pregnancy

<br>

**To prevent a recurrence** (she's had an NTD pregnancy before):
- **4 mg (4000 µg) folic acid daily**
- for the **first 4 months** of pregnancy
- and **at least 1 month before** conception

<br>

**Memory hook:** normal = **400 µg, 12 weeks**. Recurrence = **10× the dose (4 mg), 4 months**.

### 🧠 Check
A woman who had a previous pregnancy affected by spina bifida is planning another baby. What should she take, and when?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

4 mg (4000 µg) folic acid daily.

Start at least 1 month before conception and continue for the first 4 months of pregnancy.

</details>

## 11. Mandatory fortification

**US and Canada:**
**Mandatory enrichment of flour** with folic acid → **20–25% fall in NTDs**.

<br>

**Ireland, the story so far:**

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| **2006** | National Committee on Folic Acid Food Fortification **recommended** mandatory fortification of most white, brown and wholemeal **bread**. |
| **2009** | Implementation Group said there'd be **no public health benefit** to introduce it **at that time**, because: (1) intake had **already increased** through **voluntary** fortification, and (2) there was **some evidence for negative health effects**. |
| **2016** | **FSAI update (Option 1):** mandatory fortification of **bread or flour** to give about **150 µg folic acid/day** to women of childbearing age could cut NTDs by about **30%**. |

<br>

**FSAI 2016 Option 1 also says:**
- it should come with advice to **all women who could become pregnant** to take an **extra 400 µg** folic acid supplement daily
- **voluntary** fortification would **continue**

### 🧠 Check
Give the two reasons the 2009 Implementation Group gave for not bringing in mandatory folic acid fortification in Ireland.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Intake had already increased because of voluntary fortification.
2. There was some evidence for negative health effects.

</details>
