## The big idea

Vitamin D is the **sunshine vitamin**.
Your skin makes it from **7-dehydrocholesterol** when **UVB** light hits it.
Food gives some too (oily fish, fortified foods).

It isn't active straight away. It has to be switched on twice:
**liver → 25(OH)D**, then **kidney → 1,25(OH)₂D**, the active hormone.

Its main job is keeping **blood calcium** in range, which keeps **bones** healthy.

Ireland is far north and cloudy, so a lot of Irish adults are low, especially in **winter**.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**The pathway:**
7-dehydrocholesterol **→(UVB, skin)→** vitamin D3 **→(liver)→** 25-hydroxyvitamin D **→(kidney)→** 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D

| Form | Other name | Made in | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D3 | cholecalciferol | skin (or food) | the vitamin |
| **25(OH)D** | calcidiol | **liver** | **biomarker of status** |
| **1,25(OH)₂D** | calcitriol | **kidney** | **active form** |

<br>

**Two forms in food:** D2 **ergocalciferol** (plants, mushrooms), D3 **cholecalciferol** (animals, skin).

**Conversion:** **1 µg = 40 IU**

**Job:** calcium homeostasis: **↑ gut absorption, ↑ kidney reabsorption, ↑ release from bone.**

**Deficiency:** **rickets** (children), **osteomalacia** (adults).

**Toxicity:** high **blood calcium**. Never from the sun.

**Intakes:** adults **10 µg (400 IU)**, adolescents **15 µg (600 IU)**, infants 10 µg (400 IU). Older adults (FSAI 2020): **15 µg**, or **20 µg** if housebound.

## 1. Vitamin D and bones

EFSA (2014) approved this health claim:
"Vitamin D contributes to normal development of bones and teeth."

That's the headline function. Everything in §6 explains how.

## 2. Structure

- Derived from a **steroid** (cholesterol family).
- It's a **seco-steroid**: one of the steroid rings is **broken** open.
- Two forms:
  - **D2: ergocalciferol** (from plants and fungi)
  - **D3: cholecalciferol** (from animals, and made in our skin)

<br>

**Mushrooms:**
- **UV light** raises the **vitamin D2** in mushrooms a lot.
- **More surface** (e.g. sliced) = more vitamin D.
- The vitamin D **falls slowly** during storage.

## 4. Synthesis and metabolism

![Vitamin D synthesis](../img/04/l13-09.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** start top left (skin) and go down.

1. In the **skin**, **UVB** turns **7-dehydrocholesterol** (ProD3) into **preD3**, then **vitamin D3**.
2. **Dietary** D2 and D3 come in from the right. They're absorbed from **micelles** by **passive diffusion**, and carried in **chylomicrons**.
3. Vitamin D travels bound to **vitamin D binding protein (DBP)**.
4. The **liver** makes **25-hydroxyvitamin D**, 25(OH)D.
5. The **kidney** makes **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D**, 1,25(OH)₂D. This step is switched on by **PTH** and by **low phosphate**.
6. 1,25(OH)₂D acts on the **gut** (absorb more calcium and phosphorus) and **bone** (release calcium).
7. Result: **serum calcium and phosphorus** are kept steady, for bone health, metabolism and nerves and muscles.

<br>

![Vitamin D metabolism, liver and kidney](../img/04/l13-11.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** the red circles show where an –OH group gets added.

- **Liver**: **25-hydroxylase** adds –OH at position 25 → **calcidiol**, 25(OH)D.
- **Kidney**: **1-hydroxylase** adds –OH at position 1 → **calcitriol**, 1,25(OH)₂D. **Active.**
- **Kidney**: **24-hydroxylase** makes **24-hydroxycalcidiol** instead. That's the "off" route.

<br>

**The control loop:**
- Low blood calcium → **PTH** is secreted → PTH **increases 1-hydroxylase** → more active vitamin D.
- 1,25(OH)₂D **inhibits its own synthesis**, and **increases 24-hydroxylase** → more 24-hydroxycalcidiol.

That's **negative feedback**: the active hormone switches its own production off.

**D2 (ergocalciferol) goes through exactly the same steps.**

### 🧠 Check
Which organ makes the active form, and which hormone tells it to?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The kidney, using 1-hydroxylase.

PTH (parathyroid hormone), released when blood calcium is low, increases 1-hydroxylase activity.

</details>

## 5. Measuring status, and what affects it

**25(OH)D is the biomarker of vitamin D status.**
It's the circulating form that reflects both sun and diet. (1,25(OH)₂D is tightly controlled, so it doesn't show your stores.)

<br>

![Summary of vitamin D metabolism](../img/04/l13-12.jpg)

**Factors that affect status:**
- **Season** (less UVB in winter)
- **Latitude**: above **40° N or S**, winter sun is too weak. Ireland is about 53° N.
- **Skin** (darker skin makes less vitamin D from the same sun)
- **Dietary intake**

---

### 📐 Equation 1: µg and IU

**1 µg vitamin D = 40 IU**

| To go from | Do this |
|---|---|
| µg → IU | **× 40** |
| IU → µg | **÷ 40** |

**What it means:**
IU (International Units) is an older unit based on biological activity. Supplements often use it.

**Use it when:**
A question mixes µg and IU, e.g. "Is 1000 IU above the adult 10 µg?"

**Check yourself:** 10 µg = 400 IU. 15 µg = 600 IU.

### 🧠 Check
A supplement has 1000 IU. How many µg is that?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1000 ÷ 40 = **25 µg**

</details>

## 6. Vitamin D status in Ireland

![Vitamin D status in Irish adults](../img/04/l13-10.jpg)

**How to read this chart:** each bar is the % of Irish adults **below** a cut-off.

| Below | % of adults |
|---|---|
| 30 nmol/L | **6.7%** |
| 40 nmol/L | 21.9% |
| 50 nmol/L | **40.1%** |
| 75 nmol/L | 75.6% |

(Cashman et al. 2012, the National Adult Nutrition Survey, NANS.)

There's **disagreement over which cut-off** means "deficient". So the "% deficient" depends on where you draw the line.

<br>

**Season makes a big difference:**

| Below | All year | Winter | Summer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 nmol/L | 6.7% | **11.1%** | 3.4% |
| 40 nmol/L | 21.9% | 31.1% | 15.4% |
| 50 nmol/L | 40.1% | **55.0%** | 28.9% |

![Vitamin D across the year](../img/04/l13-22.jpg)

**How to read this chart:** x-axis = days from 1 March. y-axis = blood 25(OH)D.
It **rises through spring and summer** and peaks around **August/September**, then falls.

<br>

**Vitamin D and metabolic health (NANS):**

| Vitamin D quartile | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plasma vitamin D (nmol/L) | 34 | 49 | 66 | 94 |
| % with >3 metabolic syndrome factors | **13** | 11 | 11 | **7** |
| Plasma TAG (mmol/L) | 1.39 | 1.26 | 1.27 | 1.10 |
| Plasma glucose (mmol/L) | 5.36 | 5.29 | 5.35 | 4.98 |

Higher vitamin D goes with **fewer metabolic syndrome factors**, lower triglycerides and lower glucose.
It's an **association**. It doesn't prove vitamin D causes it.

<br>

**Vitamin D and death:** in a study of 9578 people, **25(OH)D below 30 nmol/L** came with **1.7× the risk of death** (all-cause).

### 🧠 Check
Why is the "% deficient in Ireland" not one single number?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

There's disagreement over the cut-off. Below 30 nmol/L it's 6.7%, below 50 nmol/L it's 40.1%.

It also changes with season: below 50 nmol/L is 55% in winter but 28.9% in summer.

</details>

## 7. Function 1: Calcium homeostasis

![Vitamin D and calcium](../img/04/l13-13.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** the tank in the middle is **plasma calcium**. The three taps fill it.

Active vitamin D keeps blood calcium up in three ways:
1. **↑ Intestinal absorption** of calcium
2. **↑ Reabsorption in the kidney** (less lost in urine)
3. **↑ Mobilisation of bone mineral** (releases calcium from bone)

The calcium then goes to **bone mineralisation** and **nerve and muscle** function.

**Memory hook:** **Gut, Kidney, Bone.** Take more in, lose less, pull from the bank.

<br>

Why this is "important for bone health": steady calcium supply is what lets bone mineralise.

---

## 8. Function 2: Other roles

**Cell differentiation**
- immune cells
- **osteoclasts** (bone cells)
- skin cells

**β-cell function** (pancreas)
- insulin **secretion and sensitivity**
- potential to improve **glucose control**

**Immune function**
- activated immune cells have **vitamin D receptors**
- enhances **antibody synthesis**
- regulates production of **cytokines**

---

## 9. Deficiency

**Rickets** (children)
- Bones **fail to calcify** normally
- So the bones **bend** (bowed legs)

**Osteomalacia** (adults)
- **Poor mineralisation**, from **demineralisation** of existing bone
- Bones are **soft, flexible, brittle and deformed**
- Most common in **women and older people**

**Memory hook:** **R**ickets = **R**unning kids. **O**steomalacia = **O**lder adults.

### 🧠 Check
What's the difference between rickets and osteomalacia?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both come from vitamin D deficiency and poor bone mineralisation.

Rickets is in children: growing bones fail to calcify, so they bend.

Osteomalacia is in adults: existing bone demineralises and goes soft, brittle and deformed.

</details>

## 10. Requirements

**RDA:**

| Group | IU | µg |
|---|---|---|
| Infants | 400 IU | **10 µg** |
| Adolescents | 600 IU | **15 µg** |
| Adults | 400 IU | **10 µg** |

**Mandatory supplementation in Irish infants: 5 µg.**

<br>

**Sun:**
5–15 minutes of sun a day, depending on **skin type, latitude and season**.
Example: average skin, noon, Miami, summer: about **6 minutes ≈ 1000 IU**.

<br>

**Older adults in Ireland (FSAI, 2020):**

| Who | Daily intake | Supplement |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy, living independently | **15 µg** | **10 µg (400 IU)** in **winter** (end Oct to March) |
| Same, but darker-skinned | 15 µg | **10 µg all year** |
| **Housebound**, little or no sun | **20 µg** | **15 µg (600 IU) all year** |

Diets should include oily fish, eggs, meat and fortified foods.
10 or 15 µg supplements are considered **safe** for older adults.

## 11. Toxicity

- Toxicity raises **blood calcium** (hypercalcaemia):
  - raised **blood pressure**
  - **calcification of soft tissue**
  - (summary slide: nausea, vomiting, weakness, kidney damage)
- **The threshold is unknown**, but:
  - adults taking supplements **>250 µg/day**
  - infants **>50 µg/day**
  - **US UL = 100 µg/day**
- **Too much sunlight does NOT cause toxicity.**
  Sunlight maxes out at about **20,000 IU**, giving 25(OH)D of **100–200 nmol/L**.

### 🧠 Check
Can you get vitamin D toxicity from sunbathing? Why or why not?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No.

Skin production tops out at about 20,000 IU, giving 25(OH)D of 100 to 200 nmol/L.

Toxicity only comes from high-dose supplements, like over 250 µg/day in adults.

</details>
