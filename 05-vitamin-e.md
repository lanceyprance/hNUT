## The big idea

Vitamin E is the body's main **fat-soluble antioxidant**.

It sits in **cell membranes** and protects the **PUFAs** (polyunsaturated fats) there from **free radicals**.
It does that by **donating** an electron (a hydrogen) to the radical, which stops a chain reaction.

Then **vitamin C** recharges it.

The more PUFA you eat, the more vitamin E you need. Handy, because PUFA-rich **oils, nuts and seeds** are also the best sources of vitamin E.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Forms (vitamers):** **tocopherols** and **tocotrienols** (α, β, γ, δ). They **can't interconvert**. **α-tocopherol** is the **most active**.

<br>

**α-Tocopherol Equivalents:**

**α-TE (mg) = α-tocopherol + 0.5 × β-tocopherol + 0.1 × γ-tocopherol + 0.3 × α-tocotrienol**

(all in mg)

<br>

**Intake:** AI = **0.4 mg α-TE per g of dietary PUFA**.

**Job:** antioxidant. Stops **lipid peroxidation** of PUFA in membranes. **Regenerated by vitamin C.**

**Deficiency (rare):** **neurological** problems, **haemolytic anaemia**.

**Toxicity (rare):** high doses raise **bleeding** risk (**interferes with vitamin K**).

**Sources:** **nuts, seeds, vegetable oils**, green leafy veg.

## 2. Forms of vitamin E

![Vitamin E vitamers](../img/05/l13-29.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:**

Every vitamer has a **ring** (left) with an –OH group, and a **tail** (right).

- **Tocopherols**: the tail is **saturated**.
- **Tocotrienols**: the tail has **three double bonds** ("trien").
- Each comes as **α, β, γ, δ**, depending on how many methyl groups are on the ring.

They **cannot interconvert**. The body can't turn γ into α.

<br>

### 📐 Equation 1: α-Tocopherol Equivalents (α-TE)

**α-TE (mg) = mg α-tocopherol + 0.5 × mg β-tocopherol + 0.1 × mg γ-tocopherol + 0.3 × mg α-tocotrienol**

| Vitamer | Counts as | Why |
|---|---|---|
| α-tocopherol | **× 1** | the most active |
| β-tocopherol | **× 0.5** | half as active |
| α-tocotrienol | **× 0.3** | about a third |
| γ-tocopherol | **× 0.1** | a tenth |

**What it means:**
Different vitamers have different **biological potency**. α-TE converts them all to "α-tocopherol worth".

**Use it when:**
You're given the vitamer content of a food or oil and asked for its vitamin E value.

**Watch out:**
Don't just add the milligrams up. Multiply each by its factor first.

<br>

**Worked example (from the slides), per 100 g:**

| | Sunflower oil | Soybean oil |
|---|---|---|
| α-tocopherol | 48.7 | 10.1 |
| β-tocopherol | 5.1 | 59.3 |
| γ-tocopherol | 0.8 | 26.4 |
| α-tocotrienol | 0 | 0 |

Sunflower = 48.7 + (0.5 × 5.1) + (0.1 × 0.8) + (0.3 × 0) = **51.33 mg α-TE**

Soybean = 10.1 + (0.5 × 59.3) + (0.1 × 26.4) + (0.3 × 0) = **80.99 mg α-TE**

### 🧠 Check
An oil has 20 mg α-tocopherol and 30 mg γ-tocopherol per 100 g, nothing else. Find its α-TE.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

α-TE = 20 + (0.1 × 30) = 20 + 3 = **23 mg**

Not 50. γ-tocopherol only counts a tenth.

</details>

### 📐 Equation 2: vitamin E needs and PUFA

**AI (mg α-TE/day) = 0.4 × g of dietary PUFA per day**

| Symbol | Means | Units |
|---|---|---|
| AI | Adequate Intake of vitamin E | mg α-TE/day |
| PUFA | polyunsaturated fat eaten | g/day |

**What it means:**
Vitamin E's job is to protect PUFA from oxidation (§4). More PUFA to protect = more vitamin E needed.

**Use it when:**
You're given a PUFA intake and asked for the vitamin E need.

### 🧠 Check
Someone eats 15 g PUFA a day. What's their vitamin E AI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

0.4 × 15 = **6 mg α-TE/day**

</details>

## 3. Absorption and transport

Same pattern as the other fat-soluble vitamins:

1. Absorbed with dietary fat in the small intestine.
2. Packed into **chylomicrons**.
3. Taken to the **liver**.
4. The liver sends it out to other tissues in **VLDL**.

## 4. Free radicals

A **free radical** is a molecule with an **unpaired electron**.
That makes it **highly reactive**.

<br>

**The cascade:**
1. The radical **steals an electron** from a stable molecule.
2. That molecule is now a **new radical**.
3. It steals from the next one...
4. A **chain reaction** of damage.

<br>

**What radicals damage:** cell membranes, DNA, RNA, proteins.

**Effects:**
- disrupt **transport** in and out of cells
- **impair cell function**
- trigger an **inflammatory response**

<br>

**Not always bad:**
White blood cells make oxygen radicals on purpose, to **kill invading pathogens**.

### 🧠 Check
Why is one free radical able to do so much damage?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It steals an electron from a stable molecule, which turns that molecule into a new radical.

That one steals from the next, so you get a chain reaction, a cascade of damage.

</details>

## 5. Antioxidants and how vitamin E works

![Antioxidant neutralising a radical](../img/05/l13-36.jpg)

**What an antioxidant does:**
- **Neutralises** free radicals
- **Donates an electron**
- **Ends the chain reaction**
- **Stays stable** itself (it doesn't become a dangerous radical)

**Definition:** a dietary antioxidant is a substance in foods that **significantly decreases the adverse effects of reactive species** on normal body function.

<br>

![Vitamin E in the membrane](../img/05/l13-33.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** it's a cell membrane, made of phospholipids with PUFA tails.
A radical attacks the PUFA tails. Vitamin E sits **inside the membrane** with them and gives up an electron to stop the damage spreading.

<br>

![Vitamin E and vitamin C](../img/05/l13-37.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:**

1. A lipid radical (R•) meets vitamin E.
2. Vitamin E **donates a hydrogen** from its **phenolic –OH** group (red circle).
3. The lipid is fixed (RH). Vitamin E becomes a **tocopheroxyl radical**.
4. That radical is **stable**, because the **aromatic ring** spreads the unpaired electron out (**resonance**). So it doesn't start a new chain.
5. **Vitamin C** reduces it **back to tocopherol**, ready to work again.

<br>

**Memory hook:** E takes the hit in the **fat** (membrane). C recharges E from the **water** side.

### 🧠 Check
After vitamin E neutralises a radical, how is it restored?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It becomes a tocopheroxyl radical, which is stable thanks to its aromatic ring.

Vitamin C reduces it back to tocopherol.

</details>

## 7. Oxidative stress and disease

![Defending against disease](../img/05/l13-39.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** it's a seesaw.

**Dietary antioxidants** (left) vs **free radicals** (right).
When radicals win, you get **oxidative stress**, which raises disease risk.

<br>

**What tips it towards radicals:**
- **low antioxidant intake** in the diet
- **age-related decline** in natural defences
- exposure to **toxins**
- **cigarettes**
- **poor nutrition**
- **stress**
- **UV light**

<br>

**Diseases linked to high free radical levels:**
cancer, cardiovascular disease (**CVD**), **cataracts**, **age-related macular degeneration**, **Alzheimer's**, **diabetes**.

## 8. Does vitamin E prevent disease?

**Epidemiology says yes:**
Studies show a clear **association** between **high blood vitamin E** and **lower CVD**.

**Trials say not really:**
**Randomised controlled trials** with **high-dose** supplements have been **disappointing**:
- small effect
- no effect
- or even **increased health risks**

<br>

**The lesson:** an association in observational studies doesn't prove that a supplement will help. People with high vitamin E may simply eat better overall.

### 🧠 Check
Observational studies link high vitamin E with less heart disease. So why don't we tell everyone to take high-dose vitamin E?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Randomised controlled trials of high-dose supplements were disappointing: small effect, no effect, or increased health risks.

An association doesn't prove cause and effect.

</details>

## 9. Deficiency and toxicity

**Deficiency (rare):**
- **neurological** problems
- **haemolytic anaemia** (red cells burst; their membranes are full of PUFA)

**Toxicity (rare):**
- high doses may **increase bleeding risk**, because they **inhibit vitamin K** (Topic 3)

<br>

**Summary slide extras:**
supports **immune function** and **skin health**.
Sources: **nuts, seeds, vegetable oils, green leafy vegetables**.
