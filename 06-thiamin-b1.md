## The big idea

The B vitamins are **water-soluble** and work mostly as **coenzymes**: helpers that let enzymes do their job.
Most of them help **release energy** from food.

**Thiamin (B1)** is the one for **carbohydrate** energy.
Its active form, **TDP**, lets the body cut CO₂ off molecules like **pyruvate** (oxidative decarboxylation).

If you're low on thiamin and eating lots of carbohydrate (or alcohol), the energy pathways jam.
Nerves and the heart suffer first: **beriberi**, and in alcoholics, **Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome**.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**B-vitamin names:** B1 thiamin, B2 riboflavin, B3 niacin, B5 pantothenic acid, B6 pyridoxine, B7 biotin, B9 folate, B12 cobalamin.

<br>

**Thiamin coenzyme:** **TDP** (thiamine diphosphate) = **TPP** (thiamine pyrophosphate). Same thing.

**Three jobs:**
1. **Energy**: oxidative decarboxylation (pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, α-ketoglutarate → succinyl-CoA, BCAA breakdown)
2. **Synthesis**: **transketolase** in the pentose phosphate pathway (DNA/RNA, NADPH)
3. **Nerve conduction**: non-coenzyme role (Na transport in nerve membranes)

**Intake:** PRI **0.07–0.1 mg per MJ** of energy (about **0.5 mg/day** on 2000 kcal, per the slide).

**Deficiency:** **beriberi** (dry = nerves, wet = heart) and **Wernicke-Korsakoff** (alcoholics).

**Sources:** **pork**, fortified cereal, wholegrains, peas.

## 2. Thiamin structure

![Thiamin structure](../img/06/l14-06.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:**

Thiamin is two rings joined together:
- a **pyrimidine ring** (left)
- a **thiazole ring** (right, it has the S: "thia")
- joined by a **methylene bridge**
- with a **hydroxyethyl side chain** on the thiazole ring

**The active coenzyme form is thiamine diphosphate (TDP)**: two phosphates added to that side chain.
TDP is also called **TPP** (thiamine pyrophosphate).

**Look at the rice:** brown 0.19 vs polished white **0.04**.
Polishing rice strips the thiamin-rich outer layer. That's why beriberi was common where polished white rice was the main food.

Enriched white bread has thiamin **added back**, so it matches wholewheat.

### 📐 Equation 1: thiamin from energy intake

Thiamin is needed to burn carbohydrate, so the requirement is set **per unit of energy**.

**PRI (mg/day) = 0.07 to 0.1 mg × energy intake (MJ/day)**

To get MJ from kcal:

**1 kcal ≈ 4.2 kJ = 0.0042 MJ**, so **MJ = kcal × 4.2 ÷ 1000**

| Symbol | Means | Units |
|---|---|---|
| PRI | Population Reference Intake for thiamin | mg/day |
| 0.07–0.1 | thiamin per MJ, from the slide | mg/MJ |
| energy intake | how much energy the person eats | MJ/day |

**What it means:**
Eat more energy (especially carbs) = need more thiamin.

**Use it when:**
You're given an energy intake (kcal or MJ) and asked for the thiamin need. Use the factor the question gives you.

**The slide's example:** 2000 kcal ≈ 8–8.5 MJ → PRI ≈ **0.5 mg** (that matches the 0.07 end of the range: 0.07 × 8 ≈ 0.56).
With 0.1 mg/MJ, 8.4 MJ gives 0.84 mg.

### 🧠 Check
Someone eats 2500 kcal a day. Using 0.1 mg/MJ, find their thiamin PRI.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

MJ = 2500 × 4.2 ÷ 1000 = **10.5 MJ**

PRI = 0.1 × 10.5 = **1.05 mg/day**

</details>

## 4. Function 1: Energy metabolism (oxidative decarboxylation)

**Oxidative decarboxylation** = remove a carboxyl group as **CO₂** and oxidise what's left.
TDP is the **coenzyme** for the enzymes that do this.

![Thiamin in energy metabolism](../img/06/l14-09.jpg)

**How to read this diagram:** it's the energy map: glycogen and triglycerides at the top, the citric acid (TCA) cycle in the middle, the electron transport chain at the bottom.
The coloured dots show which vitamin each step needs. **Yellow = thiamin.**

The three TDP enzymes (numbered on the slide):

| # | Enzyme | Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pyruvate dehydrogenase** | pyruvate → **acetyl-CoA** (into the TCA cycle) |
| 2 | **α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase** | α-ketoglutarate → **succinyl-CoA** (inside the TCA cycle) |
| 3 | **Branched-chain keto-acid dehydrogenase** | **BCAA** metabolism (leucine, isoleucine, valine) |

<br>

![Enzymes that need TDP](../img/06/l14-10.jpg)

These same enzymes also need **other B vitamins**:
- **B2** as **FAD**
- **B3** as **NAD**
- **B5** as **CoA**

So the B vitamins work as a team. Lack of one slows the whole pathway.

<br>

**Memory hook:** no thiamin → **pyruvate can't become acetyl-CoA** → carbs can't be fully burned.

### 🧠 Check
Which reaction links glycolysis to the TCA cycle, and which form of thiamin does it need?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, by pyruvate dehydrogenase.

It needs thiamine diphosphate (TDP) as its coenzyme (plus FAD, NAD and CoA).

</details>

## 5. Functions 2 and 3: synthesis and nerves

**2. Synthesis of DNA/RNA and NADPH** (coenzyme role)
TDP is the coenzyme for **transketolase**, an enzyme in the **pentose phosphate pathway**.
That pathway makes:
- **ribose** sugars for **DNA and RNA**
- **NADPH**, needed for **fatty acid synthesis**

**3. Nerve conduction** (non-coenzyme role)
Thiamin is needed for the **sodium (Na) transport protein** in **nerve membranes**.

<br>

So the three roles:

| Role | Coenzyme? | Key word |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | yes, TDP | oxidative **decarboxylation** |
| Synthesis | yes, TDP | **transketolase** |
| Nerves | **no** | **Na transport** |

## 6. Deficiency: beriberi

**Beriberi** means "weakness" ("I can't, I can't").
It comes from **chronic thiamin deficiency** plus a **high carbohydrate intake**.

<br>

**Dry beriberi → nervous system**
- weakness, stiffness and **cramps in the legs**
- as it progresses, **muscle wasting** and **paralysis** spread **upward**

**Wet beriberi → heart**
- arterioles dilate, so **fast blood flow and pulse**
- leads to **heart failure** and **oedema** (fluid swelling, e.g. in the legs)

<br>

**Memory hook:** **Wet** = **water** (oedema, heart). **Dry** = **nerves**.

### 🧠 Check
A patient has swollen legs, a fast pulse and is going into heart failure. Low thiamin is suspected. Which type of beriberi is this?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Wet beriberi: it affects the heart, with dilated arterioles, fast blood flow and pulse, heart failure and oedema.

</details>

## 7. Deficiency: Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome

The **usual way thiamin deficiency shows up in alcoholics**.
It causes lesions in the **central nervous system**.

<br>

**Two parts:**
1. **Korsakoff's psychosis**: loss of **recent memory**, confusion
2. **Wernicke's encephalopathy**: affects **vision**

<br>

**Why alcoholics are at risk** (four reasons):
1. **Low thiamin intake** (alcohol replaces food)
2. **High carbohydrate intake** (alcohol and sugars raise thiamin needs)
3. Alcohol **affects absorption** of thiamin
4. **Liver damage**

### 🧠 Check
Give two reasons alcoholics are at high risk of thiamin deficiency, and name the syndrome.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any two of: low thiamin intake, high carbohydrate intake, alcohol affects thiamin absorption, liver damage.

The syndrome is Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome (Korsakoff's psychosis: recent memory loss; Wernicke's encephalopathy: affects vision).

</details>
