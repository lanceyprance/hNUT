## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Structure and coenzymes (Core Q1)

Riboflavin is a component of two coenzymes. Name them. Where does the name "riboflavin" come from?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

FMN (flavin mononucleotide) and FAD (flavin adenine dinucleotide).

"Ribo" from ribitol, which is derived from the reduction of ribose. "Flavin" from *flavus*, Latin for yellow.

From: §1

</details>

### Q2: Functions (Core Q2)

Describe the metabolic functions of riboflavin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Its coenzymes are electron carriers in oxidation and reduction reactions, central to metabolism:
- the mitochondrial electron transport chain
- fatty acid oxidation (β-oxidation)
- amino acid oxidation
- the TCA cycle

FAD accepts electrons and becomes FADH₂, which carries them to the ETC to make ATP.

It's also needed for glutathione reductase, an antioxidant enzyme.

From: §3

</details>

---

### Q3: Deficiency (Core Q3)

Describe the signs of riboflavin deficiency.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Inflammatory lesions of the mouth:
- glossitis (inflamed tongue)
- cheilosis (cracked lips and corners of the mouth)
- stomatitis (inflamed mouth lining)

Also a lower metabolic rate, related to lipid metabolism, plus sore throat and skin disorders.

From: §4

</details>

---

### Q4: Sources (Core Q4)

Give four good food sources of riboflavin.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any four: milk, cheese and other dairy products; eggs; almonds; salmon, halibut, chicken, beef, liver; broccoli, asparagus, spinach; whole grain, enriched grain foods and fortified cereals.

From: §2

</details>

### M2: Multiple choice

Riboflavin and niacin both work as:

- A. Carboxylase coenzymes
- B. Electron carriers in redox reactions
- C. Single-carbon carriers
- D. Acyl group carriers

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

Riboflavin as FAD → FADH₂, niacin as NAD⁺ → NADH. A is biotin, C is folate, D is pantothenic acid (CoA).

### M4: Explain

Why would riboflavin deficiency lower the body's ability to get energy from fat?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Fatty acid β-oxidation needs FAD as an electron carrier.

FAD picks up electrons as FADH₂ and carries them to the electron transport chain to make ATP.

Without enough riboflavin there's less FAD, so fat oxidation slows. The slide links deficiency to a lower metabolic rate related to lipid metabolism.

From: §3, §4

</details>
