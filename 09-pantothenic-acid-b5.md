## The big idea

Pantothenic acid is **everywhere** in food. The name comes from Greek *pantos* = "everywhere".
So deficiency is **rare**.

Its whole job is to be part of **coenzyme A (CoA)**.
CoA **carries 2-carbon (acyl) groups**. Most famously as **acetyl-CoA**, the molecule that feeds the **TCA cycle**.
So B5 is **critical for ATP production**, and for **making fats, cholesterol and steroid hormones**.

## ⭐ Learn by heart from this topic

**Name:** Greek *pantos* = everywhere → **widely distributed** in foods.

**Structure:** **pantoic acid + β-alanine**. Becomes part of **coenzyme A**.

**In food:** free, or as part of CoA (**85%** of intake is CoA).

**Intake:** **AI = 5 mg/day**.

**Job:** **CoA**, a **carrier of acyl (fatty acid) groups**:
- **catabolic**: β-oxidation cuts 2-carbon pieces → **acetyl-CoA** → TCA cycle → ATP
- **synthesis**: **acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → malonyl-CoA** (step 1 of fatty acid synthesis); **cholesterol** and **steroid hormone** synthesis

**Deficiency (rare, malnutrition):** fatigue, depression, irritability, GI upset, **"burning feet syndrome"**.

## 3. Absorption and metabolism

1. In the gut, the **CoA form is broken down** to free pantothenic acid.
2. **Pantothenic acid is absorbed in the small intestine.**
3. It circulates in the blood to the **liver**, where it's metabolised, then sent to the rest of the body.
4. **Most** of it is used to **make CoA**.
5. CoA is found in **high concentrations** in the **liver, heart, kidneys and brain**.

## 4. Function: coenzyme A

**Critical for ATP production.**

CoA is needed for:
- **glucose** metabolism
- **amino acid** metabolism
- **fatty acid** metabolism
- **energy** (TCA cycle)

On the energy map (Topics 6–8), **purple dots = pantothenic acid**. They're on pyruvate → acetyl-CoA, β-oxidation, and the TCA cycle.

<br>

**CoA is a carrier of acyl groups** (like fatty acids). Two directions:

**a. Catabolic (breaking down), example: β-oxidation**
1. Fatty acids are broken down for energy by **β-oxidation**.
2. **2-carbon fragments** are cut off the end of the carbon chain.
3. Each 2-carbon fragment joins with **CoA** to form **acetyl-CoA**.
4. **Acetyl-CoA enters the TCA cycle** to produce energy.

**b. Synthesis (building up)**
1. **Long-chain fatty acid synthesis**.
   Step 1: **acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → malonyl-CoA**.
2. **Cholesterol** and **steroid hormone** synthesis.

<br>

**Memory hook:** CoA is a **delivery van** for 2-carbon pieces. It drives them **into** the TCA cycle (energy), or **onto** a growing fat chain (synthesis).

## 5. Deficiency

**Rare**, because it's in so many foods. Seen with **malnutrition**.

Symptoms are a **general failure of body systems** (it's in every energy pathway):
- **fatigue**, **depression**, **irritability**
- **gastrointestinal** stress, **vomiting**, **cramps**
- **"burning feet syndrome"**: numbness and tingling in the **hands and feet**

<br>

**Memory hook:** B**5** → **5** toes burning. Pantothenic acid = burning feet.
