## Part A: Core questions

### Q1: Synthesis and activation (Core Q1)

Describe how vitamin D is made in the skin and activated in the body. Name the active form and the biomarker of status.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. In the skin, UVB converts 7-dehydrocholesterol (via previtamin D3) to vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol).
2. Dietary vitamin D is absorbed from micelles by passive diffusion and carried in chylomicrons.
3. It travels bound to vitamin D binding protein (DBP).
4. In the liver, 25-hydroxylase makes 25-hydroxyvitamin D, 25(OH)D (calcidiol).
5. In the kidney, 1-hydroxylase makes 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol).

Active form: 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D.

Biomarker of status: 25-hydroxyvitamin D.

From: §4, §5

</details>

### Q2: Calcium homeostasis (Core Q2)

Explain how vitamin D maintains blood calcium, including how its activation is controlled.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Active vitamin D raises blood calcium in three ways:
1. increases intestinal absorption of calcium
2. increases reabsorption of calcium in the kidney (reduces excretion)
3. increases mobilisation of bone mineral

Control: low blood calcium causes PTH to be secreted. PTH increases the action of 1-hydroxylase in the kidney, so more active vitamin D is made.

1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D inhibits its own synthesis and increases formation of 24-hydroxycalcidiol (negative feedback).

From: §4, §7

</details>

### Q3: Deficiency (Core Q3)

Describe the two deficiency diseases of vitamin D, and who each affects.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Rickets** affects children. Bones fail to calcify normally, so they bend.

**Osteomalacia** affects adults, especially women and older people. Poor mineralisation from demineralisation of bone; bones are soft, flexible, brittle and deformed.

From: §9

</details>

### Q4: Status in Ireland (Core Q4)

What factors affect vitamin D status? Describe the status of Irish adults, and why the "% deficient" is debated.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Factors:** season, latitude (above 40° N/S), skin (type), dietary intake.

**Irish adults (NANS):** 6.7% below 30 nmol/L, 21.9% below 40, 40.1% below 50, and 75.6% below 75 nmol/L.

Status is worse in winter: below 50 nmol/L is 55% in winter vs 28.9% in summer.

**Debate:** there's disagreement over which cut-off defines deficiency, so the % changes a lot depending on the line chosen.

From: §5, §6

</details>

### Q5: Other functions and toxicity (Core Q5)

Apart from calcium, give three other functions of vitamin D. Then describe vitamin D toxicity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Other functions** (any three): cell differentiation (immune cells, osteoclasts, skin cells); β-cell function (insulin secretion and sensitivity, potential to improve glucose control); immune function (activated immune cells express vitamin D receptors, enhances antibody synthesis, regulates cytokines).

**Toxicity:** raises blood calcium, leading to increased blood pressure and calcification of soft tissue (also nausea, vomiting, weakness, kidney damage). The threshold is unknown, but adults taking >250 µg/day and infants >50 µg/day are at risk; the US UL is 100 µg. Excess sunlight does not cause toxicity.

From: §8, §11

</details>

### M1: True or false

1. Vitamin D2 is cholecalciferol.
2. 25-hydroxyvitamin D is made in the liver.
3. PTH increases the activity of 1-hydroxylase.
4. Too much sun exposure can cause vitamin D toxicity.
5. More Irish adults are below 50 nmol/L in winter than in summer.
6. Osteomalacia mainly affects children.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **False.** D2 is ergocalciferol. D3 is cholecalciferol. (§2)
2. **True.** By 25-hydroxylase. (§4)
3. **True.** PTH is released when calcium is low. (§4)
4. **False.** Sunlight maxes out at about 20,000 IU. (§11)
5. **True.** 55% in winter vs 28.9% in summer. (§6)
6. **False.** That's rickets. Osteomalacia is adults. (§9)

### M5: Explain

An 80-year-old woman in Dublin is housebound with almost no sunlight. Using the FSAI (2020) advice, how much vitamin D should she get each day, and what supplement should she take?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Housebound older adults with limited or no sunlight: recommended intake 20 µg/day.

She should take a daily supplement of 15 µg (600 IU), throughout the full year.

Her diet should include oily fish, eggs, meat and vitamin D-fortified foods.

### M6: Explain

Explain why vitamin D status in Ireland is worse in winter than in summer.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Most vitamin D comes from skin synthesis, which needs UVB.

Ireland is above 40° N (about 53° N). In winter, UVB is too weak at that latitude, so little vitamin D is made.

So 25(OH)D falls through winter and rises through spring and summer.

From: §5, §6

</details>

### M7: Multiple choice

In the NANS data, people in the highest quartile of plasma vitamin D (Q4) compared with the lowest (Q1) had:

- A. More metabolic syndrome factors
- B. Higher plasma triglycerides
- C. Fewer metabolic syndrome factors and lower glucose
- D. No difference

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

Q4 (94 nmol/L): 7% had more than 3 MS factors, TAG 1.10, glucose 4.98. Q1 (34 nmol/L): 13%, TAG 1.39, glucose 5.36. It's an association, not proof of cause.

From: §6
